The course concludes with a traditional examination seminar where each student present their project and receive and provide feedback to others. Actively participating in the examination seminar is a requirement for passing the course.

This fills two purposes:

1. It gives the course organizers and examiners an opportunity make sure you take full ownership of, and understand your mini-project.
2. There is generally more than one way to solve a problem and the examination seminar is an opportunity to see other students solutions. It is also an opportunity to discuss the reproducibility of your project and how it can be improved.

Each student presentation should be around 10-15 minutes (i.e. 3-5 powerpoint slides), followed by 5-10 minutes of questions and discussion. The presentation should include an overview of how you organized your mini-project. Additionally, mention if you had any challenges during the course, how you solved them and what experiences from the course you will take with you in your future work.

Some suggestions for what to put on your slides:

- A description of data you simulated, that is relvant to your research
- A flowchart of your analysis pipeline (i.e. how you organized your project)
- A quality control figure you used to assess your data
- A description of a custom function you are proud of that others may find useful
- Your main results (e.g. figure or table) or findings from your project
- A description of how you ended up with a complicated merge conflict (e.g. a git tree diagram) and how you solved it
